Nordson

Every machine in the line exists twice: photographed and drawn. Both are retouched, cut out, and sized to drop straight into whatever needs them — so nobody waits on a studio, and nothing gets redrawn twice.

One machine, twice

The drawing

Drawn from the photograph, so the two are interchangeable at any size. The drawings carry technical documentation, spec sheets, marketing, and trade-show graphics — anywhere a photograph would be too literal, too dark, or too heavy to print.

The photograph

Shot, composited, and retouched into a drop-in asset. I also worked with the engineers on the machine's form, and drew its mark — so the object, its identity, and its picture were all decided in the same room.

Why bother

An illustration is the infographic of a photograph

A photograph tells you what a machine looks like. A drawing tells you what it is — it discards the reflections and the factory floor and keeps the form, the parts, and the relationships. One is evidence, the other is explanation, and an industrial line needs both constantly.

Holding both for every unit means the request "we need the Cordis for a spec sheet by Friday" stops being a project. The asset already exists, in the right form, at the right size, cut out, on the right background. That is the whole argument: the library is the deliverable, not any single image in it.
