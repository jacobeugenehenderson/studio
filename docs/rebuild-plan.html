One operation, six scales

A full editorial rebuild, argued from a single claim: that the work is compositing, and that every piece of it is an infographic.

01

The claim the site makes

Thesis

Compositing is the through-line, and it's literal. Sources captured separately, under conditions that never coincided, made into one thing that reads as though it always was. Your own architecture doc says it: the runtime composes them.

The form is the infographic. A map is one you walk into, a QR code is one a machine reads, a rising gold bar is one about death. That gives the site a grammar — annotated, labelled, legended — derived from your practice rather than imposed by taste. Not a headline word: it carries 2012 content-marketing baggage.

Title

Creative Operations leads as the practice; Creative Director carries the level. Mirrors your CV — name huge, then title, then the three-part competency line. People recognise Creative Director but it reads light, and the point is substance. "Compositor" stays out of the title slot; it's a real job that ranks below you, and it works as the argument in the paragraph after.

02

The editorial element

One big
swing

Your brief: from the before time, when things had to look good; now they only have to look real. A 2D, textured sensibility applied to digital artifacts. Fun, alive, motion in the transitions.

The name at enormous scale, assembled from misregistered printing plates that slide into registration on load. Offset separations, visible tooth, halftone texture — flat and made rather than photoreal and rendered.

It earns its place three times. Plate and registration are the exact vocabulary of the West Elm files directly below it. Separate plates converging into one image performs the compositing thesis typographically, before a word is read. And print craft states the before-time argument in the medium rather than describing it.

Motion concentrates here and in page transitions — one orchestrated moment beats scattered effects. prefers-reduced-motion lands it pre-registered.

03

Visual system

Palette
Replaced in Rev C

Emoji-derived palette engine. Live emoji picker on the QR page. Both gone. The app is toy enough, and the mechanism was solving a problem nobody had.

Press ink and paper. A paper ground, registration black, and process inks used sparingly — which is already implied by a hero built from misregistered plates. Then each piece carries one spot ink, chosen by hand.

"Spot colour" is the correct before-time concept and does the job the emoji ramps were doing without the machinery. Nordson keeps its real inks, which sit naturally in a process system since that blue is nearly a process cyan.

CSS

All CSS in one findable place. css/tokens.css for tokens, css/site.css for everything else. No inline style attributes, no per-component blocks, no CSS-in-JS. Components style through tokens only, so a palette change is one file.

Assets

Grey slugs everywhere. Every image, photo, and video is a labelled placeholder at correct aspect ratio. Layout and motion settle before a single real file is wired, and nothing blocks on asset hunting.

QR
codes

Two, both mobile-legitimate: Ward and Picture Wrap. The page discusses the work; the code takes you to the real asset. Each PNG wraps in an <a> — phones scan, desktops click — which retires inverted-polarity risk as a dead end.

Picture Wrap's is gold on transparent, Oscars register: a citation, since In Memoriam names exactly the people that project is about. Anchors get real accessible names — "Open Picture Wrap", not "QR code".

The six, in order

Order
logic

Rank carries information, so it's numbered. West Elm leads because a stranger recognises the name and the image is beautiful — client scale and taste. Below it, scale escalates while the operation stays identical.

Recognisable client, and the eye.

SourcesRegistered. Each _Plate diptych holds the registered location plate in one half, the studio source in the other. In 20 the pool, coping, gravel and dappled light match the composite exactly.

QR engine — okQRal · Codedesk · Ward

One engine, three deployments, config as the skin. Also the fun one: light, low-commitment, quick.

EvidenceokQRal: one qr_app.js, 2,884 lines. Codedesk: the same app as five named engines. Ward's public/codedesk/: that identical file set, filename for filename. A skeptic verifies it from a directory listing in ten seconds.
The hingeqr_type_manifest.json is 19.6 KB public, 5.9 KB at Nordson. The manifest is the skin.
DemoLive generation, full type vocabulary. The one page where codes are the content — and the app is its own toy, so it needs no scaffolding.
5

Ward — ward.works

The commons, rendered as the actual place. Self-directed, nobody asking, and already a platform.

ReframedNot a map viewer with good trees. The Player carries community fortification — ticker, bulletin board — so this is neighbourhood infrastructure that happens to be drawn as the neighbourhood. That's the argument, not the tree pipeline.
Architecture authorArborist+Meteorologist ↓ pour into composeCartograph → the slab ↓ animated by runtimePlayer — ticker, bulletin board The slab is the whole environment; the Player animates it.
ArboristNot LiDAR-informed. Species library and specimen browser, low-poly sources baked to GLB with per-instance jitter, bark, seasons, morph-and-tint leaves. The .laz files mislead; I had this wrong and it's recorded now.
ProofLafayette Square extremely elaborated, Highpointe Demun heavily built, Altadena with mountains and 20,000 people. Same slab-and-player, three neighbourhoods, wildly different terrain.
NamingResolved. Platform is Ward — municipal division, and to ward is to protect. St. Louis governs by aldermanic ward, so it's locally literal. lafayette-square.com stays as the neighbourhood's own home.
Fifth
instanceThree neighbourhood domains off one slab-and-player is the okQRal/Codedesk pattern at the infrastructure level. Say it explicitly — it's checkable structural evidence.

Information design on live sources — the display is the invention.

Copy"Every picture wraps twice." Your own words.
